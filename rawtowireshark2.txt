NDAs (Non-Disclosure Agreements) are legally binding contracts that protect confidential information shared between parties.⁠Legal.thomsonreuters

Simple Explanation
An NDA (also called a confidentiality agreement) ensures that if one party shares sensitive details with another — like business plans, trade secrets, customer data, technical vulnerabilities, or test findings — the receiving party cannot disclose or misuse that information without permission.⁠Wikipedia

Key Types
Unilateral (One-way): Only one side shares secrets (e.g., a company hiring a pentester shares internal network details).
Mutual (Two-way): Both parties protect each other's information (common in partnerships or negotiations).⁠Penetration-testing
Why They Matter in Cybersecurity / Pentesting
In your field (ethical hacking, RF recon, web app testing, etc.), NDAs are very common:

Clients must share sensitive info (IP addresses, system architecture, credentials, internal processes) for you to test them.
The NDA legally binds you to keep everything confidential and not publicly disclose vulnerabilities or findings without approval.⁠Penetration-testing
It protects both sides: You avoid legal trouble, and the client protects their business.
Violating an NDA can lead to lawsuits, financial penalties, or damaged reputation.

In Your Portfolio Context
When I mentioned "without breaking NDAs," it means many real-world pentesting engagements have strict rules on what you can show publicly. That's why professionals often anonymize details, use fictionalized examples, or get explicit client permission before including case studies/screenshots in portfolios like yours.⁠Esign
